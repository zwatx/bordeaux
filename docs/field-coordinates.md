# Field coordinates and Flip

Projects pinned to `wpilib-blue-origin/2026-welded/1` use meters in the fixed blue-wall frame. `(0, 0)` is the blue-side corner beside the scoring-table guardrail. Positive X points toward red; positive Y points away from the scoring table. Heading zero points along positive X, with counterclockwise positive rotation. The welded playing rectangle is 16.541 m by 8.069 m. Planner output and BDX samples use this same frame.

The original Bordeaux map remains the display asset. `src/shared/field/imageCalibration.ts` maps the inner playing rectangle, excluding station aprons, from native pixels `(524, 95)` through `(3377, 1488)`. The raster rotates once by 180 degrees into the blue-left orientation. The grid, frame, authored features, playback and pointer inverse use the shared projection; viewport zoom and display units do not change stored meters. The frame's four mathematical corners coincide with the calibrated pixel rectangle. Raster antialiasing does not establish subpixel physical survey accuracy.

Flip is a project edit: every authored path moves to the opposite alliance within the fixed frame, using `(W − x, H − y, heading + 180°)`. Waypoints, control handles, rotation targets, turn headings and look-at points transform together. Shared-point links and routine references retain their identities. Distances, timing anchors and robot-local jiggle directions retain their meaning. Arbitrary team command arguments are opaque and are not interpreted as field coordinates.

One Undo restores all paths exactly. Two Flips restore positions while adding a full revolution to stored headings. Headings remain unnormalized so exact moving half-turn interpolation retains its direction. The shared angle wrapper tolerates floating-point noise at signed π. For stationary turns, equally short clockwise/counterclockwise half-turns default to counterclockwise; an explicit turn direction takes precedence. This tie rule is independent of how `atan2` represents the incoming and outgoing tangents.

The planning worker validates each current applied trajectory before rotating its samples and rebuilding generated route semantics, then qualifies the transformed result again before returning it. An invalid selection blocks Flip with explicit recovery. Outdated selections remain outdated. A delayed result cannot overwrite intervening edits or a different project. The normal export validation still runs on the transformed result.

Opening an unpinned legacy source or a source pinned to `bordeaux-field/1.0` performs a one-time migration. The previous raster frame used 17.548 by 8.052 units with red on the left; migration preserves normalized playing-rectangle positions while rotating both axes into the fixed blue frame. Absolute authored headings rotate by 180 degrees. A `fieldMigration` record identifies the old frame, and standalone `.path` files now persist their field pin. Historical recovery receipts authenticate their original source; the editor receives a migrated copy. Applied artifacts from the old frame retain their old identity and require review rather than receiving new authority.

BDX consumers must honor the declared coordinate schema. New blue-origin samples must not receive a second alliance flip. Historical BDX bytes retain their original frame; unknown frames must not be guessed. The original binary fixtures and planner corpus remain under their historical directories. Separate blue-origin binary evidence and the v2 synthetic corpus have independent byte digests; their results are not interchangeable with v1 measurements.

Verification includes source migration, exact projection corners, accepted-route and half-turn regressions, binary round trips, native pointer/keyboard Flip, invalid drafts, Undo/Redo, save/reopen, both unit systems and supported window sizes, and a delayed worker result after an intervening edit. Native NI execution and robot deployment are separate checks.
